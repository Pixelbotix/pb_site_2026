Industrial applications

Where can handheld 3D scanners be used?

Handheld 3D scanning helps engineering and manufacturing teams capture the geometry and surface details of physical components. The right scanner depends on part size, surface finish, required detail, accuracy needs and the intended downstream workflow.

Automotive and auto components

Capture housings, brackets, trim parts, tooling, prototypes and complex component geometry.

Mechanical engineering

Digitise machine parts, assemblies, legacy components and physical samples for engineering workflows.

Tool, die and mould

Capture tooling surfaces, mould geometry, fixtures and components for design review and reconstruction.

Industrial manufacturing

Support product development, component digitisation, maintenance and manufacturing documentation.

Reverse engineering

Use scan data as a reference for CAD reconstruction, redesign and product development workflows.

Research and education

Support engineering laboratories, academic projects, product design and applied research.

What is the difference between 3DeVOK MT Gen2 and MT Professional?

The MT Gen2 is the newer advanced model with up to 0.03 mm stated basic accuracy and additional blue laser scanning modes, including parallel and single-line modes. The MT Professional has up to 0.04 mm stated basic accuracy and combines blue laser, infrared laser and infrared structured-light modes. Compare the official specifications against your part and workflow before selecting.

Which 3DeVOK scanner supports colour 3D scanning?

The 3DeVOK MQ supports 24-bit colour texture capture. Confirm the software and workflow requirements for your intended colour-scanning application before purchase.

Can the 3DeVOK scanner be used wirelessly?

The optional 3DeVOK Airgo Wireless Handle supports wireless scanning with compatible MT and MQ scanners. Confirm model compatibility and the required wireless adapter or router configuration before ordering.
